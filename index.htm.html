De Moivre’s Theorem

What happens when you raise a complex number to a power? Spin it and see.

(cos θ + i sin θ)n = cos(nθ) + i sin(nθ)

For a number of any size, z = r(cos θ + i sin θ), the rule becomes zn = rn(cos nθ + i sin nθ). The length is raised to the power n, and the angle is multiplied by n.

Step by step (multiply z by itself n times)

De Moivre’s shortcut

Why does it work?

  1. When you multiply two complex numbers, lengths multiply and angles add. This comes from the angle-addition formulas for sine and cosine.
  2. Squaring z means multiplying z by itself. The length becomes r × r = r², and the angle becomes θ + θ = 2θ.
  3. Multiply by z again for z³. The angle becomes 3θ and the length r³.
  4. Keep going n times: the angle is nθ and the length is rⁿ. That is De Moivre’s theorem.

Try it in the simulation: set r = 1, θ = 40°, and n = 9. The angle is 9 × 40° = 360°, so z⁹ lands back on 1. The dots come full circle.

Worked example

Find (1 + i)8.
Length: √(1² + 1²) = √2. Angle: 45°. So 1 + i = √2(cos 45° + i sin 45°).
Apply the theorem: (√2)8 = 16, and the angle is 8 × 45° = 360°.
So (1 + i)8 = 16(cos 360° + i sin 360°) = 16.
